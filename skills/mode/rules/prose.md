## Prose

- Write the sentence you would say aloud to a colleague. One idea per sentence, and a short plain sentence between two dense ones is a feature.
- No em dashes, and no spaced dash splicing two clauses. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses, or a second sentence. Numeric ranges keep their dash.
- No math or logic symbols in prose: write "then", "leads to", "at least". Symbols live in code blocks.
- A term of art gets a plain-word gloss on first use, or the plain words replace it.
- Rotate rhetorical devices. The "X, not Y" contrast and the trailing rationale clause are fine once per page and a fingerprint in every paragraph. Vary certainty honestly instead of writing every sentence as law.
- Lead with the outcome, and write every summary for someone who was not watching: each file, flag or identifier gets a clause saying what it is.
